# The Gnarbler 2026 macro draft strategy

Status: review draft

## League context

- 14 teams, half-PPR, 16-round snake
- Draft slot 1
- Starters: QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX, K, DEF
- Bench: 6
- Keepers: Chris Olave in Round 4 and Quentin Johnston in Round 14
- Picks: 1; 28/29; 56/57; 84/85; 112/113; 140/141;
  168/169; 196/197; 224

This is a strategic baseline, not a rigid position script. Sleeper availability,
tier breaks, and roster construction at the current turn remain authoritative.

## Governing idea: put the better asset second

Every wrap pair crosses from an earlier round into a later round. Nobody drafts
between our two picks, so player availability is identical for both selections.
When choosing two players, take the lower keeper-priority player first and the
better long-term player second. The second player then carries the later-round
keeper cost.

"Better" means the player we would most want to keep next season, not merely the
player with the highest redraft projection. Age, role durability, breakout
ceiling, and expected keeper surplus all matter.

Exceptions:

- A keeper is already locked into the first slot at picks 56 and 196.
- Do not distort the pair when neither player is a plausible keeper, such as K
  and DEF.
- If the platform introduces an unexpected clock or transaction risk, secure
  the essential player first.

For every live pair proposal, label the players `FIRST` and `SECOND / KEEPER
PRIORITY` so the order is deliberate.

## Draft thesis

1. Start with an elite workload RB at pick 1 unless late information materially
   changes the top tier.
2. Treat WR as a volume position. The league starts 42 WRs before FLEX, and
   Quentin Johnston should be viewed as depth/upside rather than as a reason to
   stop drafting receivers.
3. Leave the first three rounds with three cornerstone players, then add Olave
   in Round 4. At least one of picks 28/29 should normally be a WR.
4. Buy a true positional edge at QB or TE only at the right tier. Do not force
   both early and hollow out RB/WR depth.
5. Aim to complete the starting offense by Round 9, then draft contingent-value
   RBs and ascending WRs rather than low-ceiling veterans.
6. Draft one QB and one TE by default. Add a second only when the starter is
   fragile, the value is exceptional, or the second player has real trade or
   keeper upside.
7. Use Round 12 on DEF, Round 13 on an upside offensive player, and Round 16 on
   K. This intentionally places the more keepable player second at picks
   168/169 and preserves Round 15 for one final upside swing.

## Checkpoints

These are outcomes to seek, not reasons to pass on a falling tier.

### After pick 29

- One elite RB
- At least one newly drafted WR
- A third cornerstone selected from WR, RB, elite QB, or elite TE

Default constructions, in order of preference:

1. RB + WR + premium TE/WR
2. RB + WR + premium QB
3. RB + WR + RB

Avoid opening RB-RB-RB unless the values are overwhelming. With three required
WR starters and a long wait to pick 56, that build creates too much receiver
pressure.

### After pick 57

- Olave added as a locked WR
- At least two reliable RB/WR starters beyond Johnston
- No more than one early onesie position unless both QB and TE represented
  unmistakable tier wins

### After pick 85

- Both starting RB slots filled or one clear RB2 plan remains
- Three credible starting WRs when Olave is included
- At least one of QB or TE solved
- No reach solely to make the lineup graphic look complete

### After pick 113

- Starting offensive lineup complete
- At least one bench RB with contingent workload upside
- At least one bench WR with role-growth or breakout upside
- QB2 or TE2 taken only by exception

### Final roster shape

The default 14 offensive players should land near one of these shapes:

- Robust skill depth: 1 QB, 6 RB, 6 WR, 1 TE
- Extra receiver depth: 1 QB, 5 RB, 7 WR, 1 TE
- Onesie insurance: 2 QB, 5 RB, 6 WR, 1 TE, or 1 QB, 5 RB, 6 WR, 2 TE

Add 1 DEF and 1 K for 16 total roster spots. Do not take both QB2 and TE2 unless
the board supplies extraordinary value; doing so leaves too few RB/WR shots.

## Position rules

### Running back

- Default pick 1 target.
- Seek two usable starters by Round 7.
- Favor backs with receiving work in half-PPR and backups who can inherit a
  large role from one injury.
- After RB2, prioritize ceiling and role contingency over veteran weekly floor.

### Wide receiver

- Take at least one at picks 28/29 unless the board produces two exceptional
  non-WR tier values.
- Olave is a starter. Johnston is a discounted upside reserve until his current
  role proves otherwise.
- Prefer target-earning ability and ascending roles; the format requires depth
  at three WR spots plus FLEX.

### Quarterback

- Take an elite difference-maker at 28/29 only if the positional edge survives
  comparison with the WR/RB tier.
- Otherwise target the best remaining starter tier in Rounds 5-9.
- In a 14-team room, do not wait past the last acceptable starter tier merely
  because the format uses one QB.

### Tight end

- Take a premium option at 28/29 only for a real tier advantage.
- If that tier is gone, target the next coherent starter tier in Rounds 5-9.
- Avoid paying for a name after the useful tier has broken; pivot to value and
  revisit the position at the next turn.

### Defense and kicker

- Plan DEF at pick 168 and K at pick 224.
- DEF can move later only if a clearly superior upside player is available and
  a usable defense is very likely to survive the 27-pick gap.
- Streamability matters more than brand name. Prefer early-season matchup and
  role stability over last year's finish.

## Pair decision procedure

At each turn:

1. Refresh Sleeper once and remove unavailable players.
2. Identify positional tier cliffs before comparing individual names.
3. Build two-player constructions, not two independent rankings.
4. Reject pairs that leave an urgent position exposed across the next 27 picks.
5. Rank the two selected players by keeper desirability.
6. Draft the lower keeper-priority asset first and the better asset second.
7. Reassess the next turn only when requested; do not automate the draft.

## Pivot rules

- Tier value beats a soft positional target; roster checkpoints constrain the
  next turn rather than forcing the current pick.
- If a QB or TE run starts after our turn, accept it. Do not prepay for a run
  unless the last acceptable player is already at our pick.
- If a run happens before our turn, take the value it pushed down rather than
  selecting the worst player from the depleted tier.
- Break ties toward younger players with clearer future role growth because the
  later pick in each pair can become a keeper asset.
- Break age/ceiling ties toward the scarcer immediate lineup need.
- Do not let bye weeks override meaningful value. Use them only as a close
  tiebreaker, especially at QB and TE.
- Revisit this document if injuries, depth-chart changes, or league keeper rules
  materially alter the assumptions.

## Live-draft interpretation

Cached ECR, ADP, and editorial rankings are evidence, not the strategy itself.
Keeper selections distort normal ADP, and a 27-pick gap makes tier survival more
important than small rank differences. At decision time, the recommendation
should state:

- the best pair construction;
- which player is intentionally selected second and why;
- the opportunity cost before the next turn;
- the pivot if either target is unexpectedly unavailable; and
- any current injury or role news that materially changes the macro plan.
